Research Interests

I study how to reduce the cost of simulating complex physical systems while retaining the accuracy needed for prediction. My work has two main strands:

  • Surrogate modeling for plasmas. In semiconductor etching, changes in pressure, RF voltage, or electrode spacing can affect how plasma is distributed near the wafer. Running a high-fidelity simulation for every combination is expensive. We compare how many simulations different models need to reach a target accuracy, and how well they predict near-wafer ion density when the reactor geometry changes.
  • Numerical methods for nonequilibrium flows. I study how to model chemical reactions and transport from continuum to rarefied regimes within one gas-kinetic framework, and how adaptive wave–particle decomposition can avoid unnecessary particle tracking in regions close to equilibrium.
